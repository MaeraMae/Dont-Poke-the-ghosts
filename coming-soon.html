The Witch's Toolkit

Resource Materials

Historical countermeasures against the supernatural, organized the way any fieldwork should be: how to guard yourself, how to fight back, and how to disappear entirely.

Defense

Warding, protecting, keeping something from crossing the threshold in the first place.

🧂

Salt

Poured across a threshold, its grains are said to bar entry to spirits who must count every one before crossing — a stalling tactic recorded from European folk magic to Japanese purification rites.

⚙️

Iron

Called "cold iron" in British and Irish tradition, forged metal was believed to repel fairies and other otherworldly beings — a horseshoe nailed above a doorway carried the same charge.

🌿

Sage

Bundled and burned, sage smoke was used ceremonially by numerous Indigenous nations for cleansing, later adopted more broadly as "smudging" against an unwanted presence.

🌳

Rowan Wood

Carved into crosses or hung over doors in Scottish and Scandinavian tradition, rowan's red berries marked it as a tree the otherworld reportedly couldn't cross.

🔔

Bells

The ringing of a consecrated bell was believed across Europe and Japan to disrupt a malevolent presence — clearing a room the way smoke clears the air.

Offense

Confronting, banishing, and — when it comes to it — destroying.

🥈

Silver

Long associated with purity, silver bullets and blades became the folkloric standard for dispatching werewolves and shapeshifters, especially in Alpine and Slavic tradition.

🔥

Fire

Treated as a universal purifier, fire was used to destroy cursed objects, exhumed remains believed to be vampiric, and structures considered beyond saving.

🪵

The Stake

Driven through the heart or pinning a body to the earth, staking was a real, documented response during the 19th-century New England vampire panic — including the Mercy Brown case.

💧

Running Water

Evil spirits and witches were widely held to be unable to cross running water, making a stream or river a literal line of last resort.

✝️

Consecrated Ground & Holy Water

Blessed by clergy, holy water and consecrated soil were used throughout Christian tradition to banish or repel malevolent spirits during exorcism rites.

Concealment

Hiding, disguising, and slipping past whatever's looking for you.

🧥

Turning Clothes Inside Out

Widespread British and Appalachian folk wisdom for escaping a will-o'-the-wisp or fairy path — a reversed traveler supposedly becomes unrecognizable to whatever is leading them astray.

🏷️

False Names

In Chinese, Appalachian, and other traditions, children were sometimes given deliberately unflattering "milk names" to make them uninteresting to spirits that might otherwise take an interest.

👞

Salt in the Shoe

A traveler's ward — a pinch of salt tucked into a boot was thought to leave a false trail no spirit could follow home.

🪞

Covering Mirrors

A mourning custom observed from the American South to Eastern Europe, meant to keep a newly departed spirit from becoming trapped in its own reflection — or from spotting the living.

🚶

Not Looking Back

From Orpheus to Appalachian graveyard custom, refusing to look back while leaving a haunted place was believed to keep whatever followed from fully fixing on you.
